The Decline Spiral

A hundred people inside one institution. Each holds a depletable account of trust. Watch what happens to the institution's capacity to fix itself as that account drains.

Epistemic status. This is an illustrative agent-based model built to make a mechanism legible, not a fitted or validated empirical model: it does not draw on real institutional data and is not evidence for the theory it dramatizes. The mechanism it formalizes (trust depletion driving exit, exit removing self-correcting capacity, and that loss accelerating further decline) is the account developed across Hirschman's exit-voice-loyalty framework and the non-decision-making and decision-avoidance literatures mapped on the reading map. Whether officials' interior trust actually behaves this way is an empirical question worth investigating, most likely through interviews; this model assumes a mechanism in order to let you explore its logic, it does not test one.

What the model does

Every round, each person's trust decays under whatever systemic pressure you set, faster if institutional capacity is already low. Below a threshold that differs for each person, reflecting that people tolerate decline differently, they either leave (if they have somewhere else to go) or voice the problem. Voice is heard with the probability you set on the responsiveness slider: heard voice repairs a little trust and a little capacity; ignored voice costs trust with nothing gained. Every exit permanently removes a slice of the institution's capacity to self-correct: the mechanism that makes this a spiral rather than a steady state, since fewer people left with standing to fix things means faster decline for whoever remains.

Try pushing responsiveness to zero and watch how quickly voice becomes indistinguishable from silence. Try raising the share with outside options and watch the same decline pressure produce a much faster spiral: capacity fails not because pressure increased, but because the people most able to leave did.
