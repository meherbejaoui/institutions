# References and verification trail

Every citation used on this site (mainly on the [reading map](reading-map/)) was checked
against a publisher page, indexing record, or journal listing before publication. This file
records what was checked and how, so the trail is auditable rather than asserted.

No citation on this site was generated from memory alone and left unverified. Where sources
disagreed on a detail, that disagreement is recorded here rather than silently resolved.

---

**Bachrach, Peter, and Morton S. Baratz. "Two Faces of Power." *American Political Science
Review* 56, no. 4 (1962): 947–952.**
Verified via JSTOR's volume index (`jstor.org/stable/i333610`) and a Cambridge Core citation
record for the article, both of which agree on volume, issue, year, and page range.

**Bachrach, Peter, and Morton S. Baratz. "Decisions and Nondecisions: An Analytical
Framework." *American Political Science Review* 57, no. 3 (1963): 632–642.**
Verified via a Cambridge Core citation record for the article, which agrees with the
BibBase and Semantic Scholar listings on volume, issue, and page range.

**Hood, Christopher. *The Blame Game: Spin, Bureaucracy, and Self-Preservation in
Government.* Princeton, NJ: Princeton University Press, 2011.**
Verified directly against the Princeton University Press book page (ISBN 978-0-691-12995-2),
which lists the 2011 publication date and full subtitle.

**Bach, Tobias, and Kai Wegrich, eds. *The Blind Spots of Public Bureaucracy and the
Politics of Non-Coordination.* Cham: Palgrave Macmillan, 2018.**
Verified against the Springer Nature Link book page (`doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-76672-0`)
and Amazon listings for both the hardcover (2018) and softcover (2019) editions. The
introductory chapter cited here, "Blind Spots, Biased Attention, and the Politics of
Non-Coordination," is listed on the same Springer page as chapter 1 of the volume, authored
by the editors. Cited on this site as 2018, matching the original hardcover / DOI
registration date; a 2019 softcover reprint also exists.

**Pressman, Jeffrey L., and Aaron Wildavsky. *Implementation: How Great Expectations in
Washington Are Dashed in Oakland.* Berkeley: University of California Press, 1973.**
Verified against a Cambridge Core review of the book and the University of California Press
catalog page (ISBN 9780520053311), both confirming publisher and year. The book's full
subtitle is considerably longer than what's practical to quote in running text; the shortened
form used here is the one the University of California Press's own current catalog listing
uses.

**Anderson, Christopher J. "The Psychology of Doing Nothing: Forms of Decision Avoidance
Result from Reason and Emotion." *Psychological Bulletin* 129, no. 1 (2003): 139–167.**
Verified volume, issue, and year via SSRN, ResearchGate, and Tilburg University's research
portal listings, all of which agree. **Open discrepancy:** the closing page number is given
as 139–167 by some listings and 139–166 by others (e.g., a Scientific Research Publishing
reference record); direct access to the APA PsycNet and DOI records to resolve this was
blocked by this session's network egress policy. This site cites 139–167 as the more commonly
listed range but flags the one-page discrepancy here rather than presenting either figure as
certain.

**Hirschman, Albert O. *Exit, Voice, and Loyalty: Responses to Decline in Firms,
Organizations, and States.* Cambridge, MA: Harvard University Press, 1970.**
Verified against the Harvard University Press book page, the Internet Archive catalog
record, and Wellcome Collection's library record, all of which agree on the full subtitle
("Responses," plural) and 1970 publication. One Cambridge Core review citation renders the
subtitle as "Response" (singular), which appears to be a typographical variant rather than
the book's actual title.

## What was not verifiable in this session

Direct fetches to several academic domains (doi.org, psycnet.apa.org, papers.ssrn.com,
semanticscholar.org) were blocked by this session's network egress policy, so verification
above relies on search-result snippets and the secondary listings that were reachable
(publisher catalog pages, Cambridge Core, JSTOR's public index, Internet Archive, Springer
Nature Link) rather than the primary record in every case. Where that left a genuine gap —
the Anderson page range — it is disclosed above rather than resolved by guessing.
